\begin{abstract}
Coding agents are proliferating as chat loops, plugin kernels, and meta-wrappers around existing CLIs. The scarce resource is not another persona or sandbox flag. It is a correct concurrent \emph{software-change transaction}: who may mutate an exact repository subject, which incarnation owns that write, what evidence names that subject, and whether an ambiguous remote effect may be retried. Bullet Farm is a local-first transaction processor for software changes and model cognition in which models may propose typed patches but cannot grant write, verification, or merge authority. This preprint states that architecture, then inventories exactly what exists on 2026-08-25 against the family's own evidence ladder (component, synthetic, transaction, live, release): every implemented boundary reported here is a commit-bound \emph{component} proof; no transaction, live, or release proof exists; all 26 family release gates are blocked. It states a threat model that separates implemented boundaries from designed ones, describes precisely what two bounded TLC models do and do not check, presents evolutionary multi-agent control as a design rather than a result, and compares the authority contract---not performance---with Gas Town~/~Gas City, DeepSeek Harness, Omnigent, and single-agent harnesses at pinned commits. A reproducibility appendix lists the commands and outputs behind every number, and a red-team appendix answers or concedes the ten strongest attacks on the paper's claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The last two years produced a crowded market of coding agents: protocol-grade CLIs, IDE wrappers, hosted pull-request bots, research SWE loops, multi-agent orchestrators, plugin kernels, and meta-harnesses that wrap the previous categories~\cite{claudeCode,codexCli,cursorAcp,antigravity,gastownReadme,dshReadme,omnigentBlog}. Those systems differ in user experience and in how much of the model loop they own. They largely agree on an implicit completion rule: if a session looks done, a terminal is idle, a process exited zero, or a pull request opened, the work is complete.

That rule fails under concurrency. Public Gas Town and Gas City issues show the same family of faults repeating under load: two agents share one working tree and clobber uncommitted edits~\cite{gascity1181}; a timeout after a server-side commit mints duplicate live workflows and three writers enter one checkout~\cite{gascity5511}; a background \texttt{git checkout} against the town-root \texttt{.git} deletes operational files~\cite{gastown4094}; a session that died mid-task leaves its work claimed with no signal~\cite{gastown3584}; a patrol step classifies every live worker as dead immediately before a forced worktree removal~\cite{gastown4737}. Those reports are not a verdict that Gas Town is a weak product. They are evidence that a role/session orchestrator is the wrong abstraction for adversarial multi-writer Git~\cite{gastownAudit}.

DeepSeek Harness and Omnigent improve different layers. DeepSeek treats every capability---model, tools, sandbox, session, and UI---as a Cordis plugin and makes the append-only session log the source of model-visible context~\cite{dshArch}. Omnigent sits \emph{above} Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and other CLIs, adding OS sandboxes, an L7 credential proxy, contextual policies, and live collaboration~\cite{omnigentReadme,omnigentBlog,omnigentPolicies}. Both are real advances. Neither binds write authority to an incarnation-fenced Attempt, evidence to an exact Candidate, or remote integration to a reconciled effect receipt. A plugin swap or a wrapped harness can still let the model run \texttt{git}.

Bullet Farm inverts the authority model~\cite{bulletOverview,adr0001}. The designed law is compact:

\begin{quote}
Many agents may reason. Multiple isolated Variants may compete. Exactly one incarnation-fenced Attempt may write within each Variant. Every consequential external mutation is brokered. Every proof names its exact subject. The repository remains sovereign.
\end{quote}

The contribution of this preprint is fivefold, and each item is bounded by the evidence class it carries (Section~\ref{sec:rules}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item It states that law as a five-plane transaction with typed subjects, not as a prompt convention (Sections~\ref{sec:arch} and~\ref{sec:bulletgit}) \ecD.
\item It reduces the public Gas Town / Gas City issue corpus and the Git documentation to a closed list of missing \emph{bindings}, and maps each to a rule and to an honest implementation status (Sections~\ref{sec:failures} and~\ref{sec:resolve}) \ecO.
\item It reports \emph{exactly} which components have commit-bound receipts as of the 2026-08-25 reconciliation and which product gates are blocked, so a reader cannot confuse a green crate test with a release (Section~\ref{sec:inventory}) \ecC\ \ecB.
\item It states a threat model that lists implemented boundaries separately from designed ones, describes what two bounded TLC models encode and omit, and presents evolutionary control as design (Sections~\ref{sec:threat}, \ref{sec:formal}, and~\ref{sec:evo}).
\item It gives a falsification section, a reproducibility appendix with commands and observed outputs, and a red-team appendix that answers or concedes the ten strongest attacks on its own claims (Section~\ref{sec:eval}, Appendices~\ref{app:repro} and~\ref{app:redteam}).
\end{enumerate}

This paper does not claim that Bullet Farm is faster, cheaper, safer, or more autonomous than any named system. No public comparison is valid until the same receipt-bearing corpus runs on pinned upstream commits~\cite{bulletSnapshot}. The family's own historical adjudication scored the \emph{paper} architecture 96.4/100 and its implementation maturity about 0.5/10~\cite{potentialDraft,centerrailRedteam}; an earlier, separately produced draft is recorded but not distributed~\cite{specPaper}. The maturity number is now too low for many components and still correct for the connected product.

Three reading rules follow. Second, an exit code, a model statement, an HTTP~2xx, a branch push, or a pull request is never evidence by itself, and \texttt{UNKNOWN}, timeout, zero tests, unsupported, skipped, or flaky never equals \texttt{VERIFIED}~\cite{bulletRelease,bulletTesting}. Third, a newer commit invalidates a receipt until the mapped gate is replayed; the subjects this paper reports are those in Appendix~\ref{app:pins}, and the working tree that compiled this PDF may be newer.

\section{A Taxonomy of Coding Agents}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Treating ``coding agents'' as one category hides the authority question. We distinguish six families by \emph{who writes the repository} and \emph{what signal is allowed to mean done}. Everything in this section is \ecO: dated observation of public documentation, pinned in Appendix~\ref{app:pins}.

\subsection{Protocol-Grade Single Agents}

Claude Code (bidirectional stream JSON), Codex (App Server JSON-RPC over stdio JSONL), Cursor (Agent Client Protocol), and Google Antigravity (\texttt{agy} structured headless mode) are the four V1 adapter targets~\cite{adr0001,claudeCode,codexCli,cursorAcp,antigravity,acpSpec}. On 2026-08-25, environment-cleared local \texttt{--version}/\texttt{--help} probes of absolute binaries observed Claude Code 2.1.243, Codex CLI 0.149.1, Cursor Agent 2026.08.11-e8db854, and \texttt{agy} 1.1.19, and recorded each executable's SHA-256~\cite{adr0001}. Those observations are inventory, not certification. Cursor's required ACP entry point remained \texttt{UNKNOWN} on that host; print/stream-JSON modes must not be substituted~\cite{adr0001}. Each of these products is a capable single-agent harness. The model, or a tool the model invoked, writes the workspace. A ``plan'' flag or a vendor sandbox is defense in depth, not a foreign write gateway.

\subsection{IDE and CLI Wrappers}

Aider, Cline, Continue, Goose, and Roo Code wrap an editor or terminal around one or more models~\cite{aider,cline,continue,goose,roo}. They differ in UX, MCP adoption~\cite{mcpSpec}, and how much Git they automate. They share the user's checkout as the writable world. Isolation, if any, is a worktree, a subdirectory, or an honor-system ignore list.

\subsection{Research SWE Loops}

OpenHands, SWE-agent, Agentless, and AutoCodeRover optimize for SWE-bench-style issue resolution~\cite{jimenez2024swebench,openhands,wang2024openhands,yang2024sweagent,agentless,autocoderover}. Their contribution is the agent--computer interface and the evaluation harness. Completion is a resolved issue or a passing hidden test, not a protected integration onto a sovereign default branch under an independent attestor.

\subsection{Hosted Change Agents}

GitHub Copilot's coding agent, Jules, Devin, and Kiro accept a task and return a pull request or a hosted workspace~\cite{copilotAgent,jules,devin,kiro}. The PR is the product. That is a legitimate remote-collaboration contract. It is not an incarnation-fenced local transaction: retries, reviewer independence, and evidence staleness after rebase are delegated to the host's merge queue and to human review.

\subsection{Orchestrators}

Gas Town is a Go workspace manager for many Claude, Codex, Copilot, Gemini, or Cursor agents~\cite{gastownReadme,gastownV121}. Its README requires tmux~3.0+ for \texttt{gt up} and the tmux-backed roles, Beads (\texttt{bd}) 0.57.0+, Dolt, and ICU4C development headers for source builds; it persists work in git-backed \emph{hooks} (worktrees), coordinates via Beads/Dolt, and lands work through a bisecting merge queue called the Refinery~\cite{gastownReadme,hoare2014notrocket}. Roles---Mayor, Witness, Deacon, Dogs, polecat, Refinery---are operationally legible. Additional product machinery is easy to underestimate: convoys bundle beads; molecules instantiate formulas; a watchdog hierarchy (Witness, Deacon, Dogs) patrols health; a scheduler caps polecat dispatch (\texttt{max\_polecats}); Seance recovers predecessor session logs; Wasteland federates towns~\cite{gastownReadme}. Gas City generalizes the same factory into Agent, Bead, Formula, Rig, Pack, and Event; the dated competitor snapshot records typed run/session APIs, warm projections, formula-v2 retry/fan-out/drain controls, degraded storage outcomes, and release artifacts with checksums, SBOMs, and attestations in v1.4.1~\cite{gascityV141,gascityMain,bulletSnapshot}. The product leverage is real. The authority model is not: liveness is a terminal multiplexer, isolation is a worktree or an optional pack script, and the fleet can be the merge authority. Bullet Farm should be measured against those product properties---durable work, role flexibility, fan-out receipts, provider plurality, health reconciliation, and release evidence---not against a cartoon of ``tmux and vibes''~\cite{bulletSnapshot}.

\subsection{Plugin Kernels}

DeepSeek Harness (\texttt{dsh}) is a developer-preview agent runtime whose doctrine is ``everything is a plugin''~\cite{dshReadme,dshSite}. Cordis mounts model adapters, tools, sandboxes, storage, loops, scheduling, and the UI into one context~\cite{cordis,dshArch}. A running instance is a plugin tree composed from bundles and ordered patches. The session log is the source of model-visible context: anything the model sees must be reconstructable from that log. A turn is zero or more steps; each step is one model request plus the tools it calls; \texttt{agent/pre-step} may rewrite or reject claimed messages; tool execution is a \texttt{tools/pre-execute}, \texttt{execute}, \texttt{post-execute} waterfall~\cite{dshArch}. Standard mode gives the model a file editor, a shell, search, skills, and subagents; Code mode lets the model orchestrate tool rounds in TypeScript; Minimal mode keeps bash and \texttt{str\_replace\_editor}; Creator mode inspects the runtime to author presets~\cite{dshSite}. Experimental Agent Teams add a roster, task board, and mailbox over continuable subagents. This is an excellent composition kernel for a \emph{single} agent product. It is not a transaction kernel. Filesystem and subprocess providers share one execution world, so pointing them at a remote sandbox moves Bash and PTY together---and the model still writes files. A durable session event is not a Candidate.

\subsection{Meta-Harnesses}

Omnigent is an open-source meta-harness, with a Databricks-operated deployment, that wraps Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode, Hermes, Pi, Antigravity, and custom YAML agents~\cite{omnigentReadme,omnigentV010,omnigentBlog}. A runner exposes a uniform session API. A server stores policies and sharing. Native Linux wrappers use bubblewrap; macOS uses seatbelt; an L7 egress proxy can attach credentials so the sandbox never holds the real token~\cite{omnigentReadme,omnigentPolicies}. That credential-proxy design is one of the strongest OS-isolation stories in this survey. Native terminal wrappers still use tmux. The wrapped harness still performs Git. A policy can deny, transform, or pause a tool call; it cannot mint a Candidate or invalidate evidence when the branch moves.

\subsection{Shared Writable Identity}

Git worktrees share one object store, one set of refs, and---unless extra machinery exists---one index and one hook namespace~\cite{gitdocs}. Gas City issue~1181 records implicit pool agents resolving every session to the rig root: two parallel slings clobber uncommitted edits~\cite{gascity1181}. Gas Town issue~4094 records background polecat operations running \texttt{git checkout} against the town-root \texttt{.git}, deleting untracked operational files such as \texttt{mayor/rigs.json} and Dolt manifests~\cite{gastown4094}. A worktree is a checkout convenience. It is not a private repository.

\texttt{git clone --reference} without \texttt{--dissociate} writes \texttt{objects/info/alternates} into a borrowed store. A later \texttt{git gc --prune=now} on the mirror can unlink objects a live workspace still names~\cite{gitdocs,potentialDraft}. CoW reflinks without a documented fallback have the same ownership question.

\subsection{Hostile Configuration and Inherited Credentials}

A repository-local or inherited Git config can install filters, aliases, include files, URL rewrites, credential helpers, hooks, and \texttt{protocol.file} transports that execute host code or exfiltrate bytes during an ordinary \texttt{checkout} or \texttt{diff}~\cite{gitdocs,gitArch}. Case-folded path collisions on a case-insensitive volume, alternate data streams, and symlink traversal are the same class: Git will write something the operator did not grant. Model-authored \texttt{git} in a process that inherited \texttt{GIT\_*}, \texttt{SSH\_AUTH\_SOCK}, or a forge token is a credential and integrity failure, not a convenience. Compile-time code---Rust \texttt{build.rs}, proc-macros, TypeScript transformers---executes before any test ``runs''~\cite{potentialDraft}. Sandboxing only the test process is insufficient. Detached HEAD, an active sequencer (\texttt{MERGE\_HEAD}, \texttt{REBASE\_HEAD}), and a dirty index shared across worktrees convert ordinary ``just commit'' steps into lost or mis-attributed work~\cite{gitdocs}.

\subsection{Control Plane in Git}

Git is a poor coordination database for renewable leases, heartbeats, quota reservations, and unsettled remote effects~\cite{gitRole,gray1981tp}. Beads-on-Dolt-in-Git is an inventive store and an operationally heavy one: install depends on Dolt, ICU4C, and \texttt{bd}~\cite{gastownReadme}, and the timeout-after-commit class appears under load~\cite{gascity5511}. Putting the live ledger in the same bytes the agents edit couples control-plane availability to repository mutation.

\subsection{Inferred Completion and Unsafe Liveness}

Gas Town's Problems view names the state ``Zombie: Dead tmux session''~\cite{gastownReadme}; the multiplexer is the liveness oracle. The corpus shows both failure directions. Issue~3584 reports polecat sessions dying mid-task with no notification while the work stays hooked~\cite{gastown3584}. Issue~4737 reports a patrol cleanup step whose liveness check classifies every dog as dead and would force-remove live worktrees, held back only by an accident of layout~\cite{gastown4737}. Issue~4091 reports the refinery aliveness check trusting stale pid files so dead refineries are never respawned~\cite{gastown4091}. A timeout after Dolt committed server-side is classified transient and retried, yielding up to three complete workflows from one \texttt{gc sling}~\cite{gascity5511}. Hosted agents treat PR-opened as done. Plugin kernels treat turn-end as done. In every case, transport success or process presence is painted as domain truth.

\subsection{Evidence Not Bound to an Exact Subject}

A green CI result that names a branch, a worktree path, or a session ID is not evidence about a rebase, a repair commit, or a merge-group composition. Ordinary Git commits have no stable Change identity across rewrite~\cite{gitdocs,jjvcs}. After the branch moves, yesterday's green is a different subject. Merge queues that do not invalidate overlapping evidence create silent stale-green admission; queues that invalidate every queued candidate recreate a livelock when arrival rate exceeds verification time~\cite{potentialDraft}.

\subsection{Destruction Without Preservation}

Cleanup, GC, polecat reuse, and ``nuke'' operations that target a name, path, or inferred age destroy the only copy of uncommitted work~\cite{gastown4094,gastown4737,gastownAudit}. Preservation must bind an exact incarnation and a sealed artifact \emph{before} deletion eligibility.

\section{Bullet Farm Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

Bullet Farm is a split family: a public hub (\texttt{bullet-farm}), a control-plane kernel, a Git capability daemon, and a projection-only portal~\cite{bulletOverview}. Jeryu is the source forge and the mandatory local effect adapter. GitHub is a separately configured effect adapter, never source authority. Unless a sentence carries another tag, this section is \ecD.

\subsection{The Software-Change Transaction}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{Mission}\rightarrow\mathrm{GraphRevision}\rightarrow\mathrm{Variant}\rightarrow\mathrm{Attempt}\\
&\rightarrow\mathrm{Proposal}\rightarrow\mathrm{Candidate}\rightarrow\mathrm{Evidence}\\
&\rightarrow\mathrm{Effect}\rightarrow\mathrm{Integration}\rightarrow\mathrm{Observation}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

A Mission is a durable objective plus constraints. A Graph Revision is an immutable package decomposition. A Variant is one hypothesis with an immutable role, routing, context, and configuration snapshot. An Attempt is one execution incarnation under a unique lease and a never-reused fence. A Proposal is typed patch data. A Candidate is a provenance-bound immutable phenotype created only by BulletGit. Evidence is an independently reproduced result over that exact Candidate and admitted gate definitions. An Effect is a brokered remote mutation with read-back. Integration is protected-ref admission by the forge. Observation is post-merge survival. None of those words is a prompt label. Each is a typed subject with an exact identifier~\cite{bulletOverview,bulletEvo}.

ADR~0003 separates control, execution/repository write, independent verification, effect/attestation/integration, and observation/audit~\cite{adr0003}. Compromise of the portal, a provider, or a broker must not manufacture independent evidence once those principals exist as distinct identities. ADR~0003 \emph{requires} distinct OS identities on one host and SPIFFE-compatible identities later; that separation is not a current receipt. Production processes still share a UID, and same-UID path-based Git races remain an open setup boundary~\cite{bulletRelease}. The evolutionary control document defines roles as capability envelopes---Planner, Implementer, Critic, Verifier, Effect broker---not as personas that carry credentials~\cite{bulletEvo}. Online learning, larger councils, and distributed execution are Phase~9--10 and are out of V1~\cite{bulletEvo}.

\subsection{Three Sovereign Truths}

A common instinct is to put missions, leases, and evidence into Git because the product is about Git. That collapses two badly matched stores~\cite{gitRole}. Bullet Farm splits canonical truth:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Kernel ledger} (SQLite WAL today; PostgreSQL is a refused scaffold~\cite{kernelArch}) owns mutable authority: leases, fences, commands, policy snapshots, outbox, event log.
\item \textbf{BulletGit} owns immutable engineering subjects: Changes, Candidates, checkpoints, journals, proof roots.
\item \textbf{Forge} (Jeryu, optionally GitHub) owns remote refs, required checks, and the merged target SHA.
\end{itemize}

Cross-boundary values come from generated \texttt{bullet-wire} contracts: RFC~8785 canonical JSON, domain-separated BLAKE3, full 256-bit IDs, algorithm-tagged Git OIDs, and PASETO v4.public envelopes~\cite{rfc8785,paseto,adr0005,bulletClosure} \ecC. Prose and TypeScript types do not define the wire.

\subsection{Providers Propose}

ADR~0001 is the execution-mode decision~\cite{adr0001}. Every provider turn is read-only inside a private workspace generation. The only write input is a validated \texttt{PatchProposal}: schema identity, producing Attempt, base checkpoint, per-path preimages, and policy-admitted \texttt{gate\_ids}. Provider text such as \texttt{tests\_to\_run} or a shell command is never argv. Runner resolves gate IDs through a trusted catalog \ecC. Zero tests, timeout, skipped, flaky, unsupported, and \texttt{UNKNOWN} never equal \texttt{PASS}. At most two bounded repair turns may see typed gate results.

Public mutations use authenticated \texttt{POST /v1/commands}, initially return \texttt{202 PENDING}, and reconcile through \texttt{PENDING}$\mid$\texttt{APPLIED}$\mid$\texttt{VERIFIED}$\mid$\texttt{FAILED}$\mid$\texttt{UNKNOWN}~\cite{bulletClosure} \ecC. Transport success never implies verification. The Portal renders only those phases and treats watermark mismatch, sequence gaps, and timeouts as \texttt{UNKNOWN} or \texttt{STALE}, never as an empty success list~\cite{portalArch} \ecC.

\subsection{Why the Bindings Are the Product}

Other systems can copy a role name in a week. They cannot copy a binding without changing what an agent is allowed to \emph{be}. A Mayor that still infers liveness from tmux is a persona. A fence that is checked inside the ledger transaction at the effects gateway is a binding~\cite{kleppmann2016locking}. A plugin that swaps the filesystem provider is composition. A daemon that is the only process allowed to apply a scoped patch is a binding. A contextual policy that pauses a tool call is governance. An Evidence record that becomes invalid when \texttt{head} moves is a binding.

That is the source of leverage. As models improve, a harness that lets the model write Git gets more destructive more quickly. A transaction processor that lets the model propose gets more useful more quickly, because the same fence, Candidate, and verifier contracts apply to a weaker economy model and to a frontier council. The evolutionary document states the same rule for search: hard constraints are filters, never terms a high score may offset, and the optimizer cannot see or learn away the safety envelope~\cite{bulletEvo}. V1 records routing provenance and does not learn online.

\subsection{A Worked \#5511-Class Fault}

Issue~5511 is the canonical timeout-after-commit race~\cite{gascity5511,potentialDraft}. Under load, \texttt{bd create --graph} is killed at the runner deadline after Dolt has already committed. The client sees a timeout, classifies it transient, and re-applies. The result is up to three live workflows and three writers in one checkout.

Bullet Farm's designed handling is mechanical:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Graph materialization is one serializable insert keyed by content hash. A replay returns the existing graph; it does not mint a sibling \ecD.
\item The command that requested materialization is recorded with an idempotency key~\cite{idempotencyKey,helland2012idempotence}. An ambiguous timeout becomes \texttt{UNKNOWN}, not a retry \ecC.
\item A resolver may adopt a remote or ledger subject only when its identity equals this Attempt's desired state. Otherwise the subject is \texttt{ORPHANED} and quarantined \ecD\ (modelled in \texttt{EffectCheck}, Section~\ref{sec:formal}).
\item Even if two Attempts exist, each requires its own never-reused fence and its own private clone. They cannot share a cwd or a \texttt{.git} \ecC\ (lease/fence and private clone are components; their composition is not).
\item A writer that outlives its lease cannot push, comment, or settle: the fence is presented at the effects gateway and rejected inside the ledger transaction \ecD.
\end{enumerate}

Items~2 and~4 have component proofs. Items~1, 3, and~5 are specified and are not a live path at HEAD. The point of the walkthrough is not to claim the incident is closed in production. It is to show that the missing primitive is identity-exact adoption under ambiguity, not a smarter Mayor prompt.

\subsection{Evidence Tiers}

Writer output cannot satisfy an independent tier~\cite{adr0001,bulletEvo}. The designed ladder is:

\begin{itemize}
\item E0 --- untrusted provider or author claim;
\item E1 --- writer-visible gates on the author's workspace;
\item E2 --- clean independent reconstruction of the exact Candidate;
\item E3 --- hidden or historical holdout, plus hostile suites the author cannot weaken;
\item E4 --- human review.
\end{itemize}

The implemented verifier produces typed E2-shaped results for one fixture gate over exact base/head/tree subjects~\cite{bulletClosure} \ecC. Multi-gate aggregation, executable-digest admission, and E3 holdout custody remain open \ecD. That is the correct partial result: the identity rule exists before the gauntlet is populated.

\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{Adversary and Scope}

We assume a single-user, loopback V1 host~\cite{bulletClosure}. The adversary is not a nation-state on the wire. The adversary is (i)~a frontier model that will try to run shell, Git, and forge tools; (ii)~a second concurrent Attempt that shares a name, path, or session identity; (iii)~a crashed or partitioned runner that retries after an ambiguous timeout; (iv)~hostile repository content (hooks, filters, \texttt{build.rs}, proc-macros, \texttt{.gitattributes}) and repository-resident instruction files the provider will read before any proposal exists~\cite{greshake2023injection}; (v)~a compromised or confused portal that wants to paint \texttt{UNKNOWN} green; and (vi)~an operator who would rather flip an environment variable than mint a lease-bound grant.

Out of scope for V1, and therefore not silently promised: multi-tenant SaaS, remote untrusted runners, cross-repository sagas~\cite{garcia1987sagas}, online routing learners, a compromised Kernel ledger or \texttt{bullet-gitd} process (either is total loss on a single-user host), and a formal proof that physical side effects are exactly-once. Those absences are named so a red team cannot treat them as implied.

\subsection{Trusted Computing Base (Design)}

The designed trusted computing base for an admitted write is the Kernel ledger transaction, the signed launch-grant verifier, the egress-isolation receipt, \texttt{bullet-gitd}'s final-authority checker, and the forge's required checks \ecD. The model, the Portal, MCP servers, vendor sandboxes, and tmux are untrusted. Confused-deputy discipline is why a portal, an MCP server, or a vendor sandbox must never inherit forge credentials~\cite{hardy1988deputy}. Today no admitted write exists, so the TCB is a specification of what a future \ecT\ receipt must exercise.

\subsection{Implemented Boundaries}

Each item below is \ecC: a named commit and mapped tests exist (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). None composes into a transaction.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fail-closed argv gate.} The supervised argv boundary enforces a kill switch, a worktree/tmux deny list, the exact admitted executable, and default refusal of live provider programs; supervision kills the POSIX process group on exit, crash, cancellation, heartbeat loss, or deadline~\cite{kernelArch}.
\item \textbf{Provider process admission.} One absolute canonical executable is compared to its BLAKE3 digest and a fresh runtime probe; a unique mode-0700 \texttt{HOME} is created; only exact policy-listed OAuth files are copied, after digest and symlink checks, as mode~0400; the child environment is built from locale hints plus \texttt{HOME}/\texttt{TMPDIR}/\texttt{XDG} paths, so host \texttt{PATH}, SCM, cloud, SSH, and API secrets are not inherited; canary scanning covers environment, stdout, stderr, normalized events, and the validated proposal~\cite{kernelArch,adr0007}.
\item \textbf{Signed launch grants.} A PASETO v4.public token binds the durable active lease (Mission, repository, graph revision, work package, Variant, Attempt, fence, runner epoch, workspace nonce), the evaluated admission (path, digest, profile, protocol), the policy snapshot digest, a budget reservation, and a single-use nonce; audience \texttt{provider-runner}, operation \texttt{launch-provider}, TTL~$\le 15$\,s; minted in-process from an operator-held mode-0600 key with no HTTP route; the verifier clears only \texttt{SIGNED\_ADMISSION\_UNAVAILABLE}~\cite{adr0011,adr0005}.
\item \textbf{Linux egress isolation.} The provider runs in a fresh user+network namespace with an nftables default-drop ruleset permitting only loopback and TCP to a host-side allow-listing CONNECT proxy; eight in-namespace probes (direct internet, host forge port, host decoy port, DNS over TCP and UDP, proxy reachable, proxy-disallowed host, proxy-allowed path) must produce exactly their expected refusals and admissions before the child starts; the sealed receipt clears only \texttt{EGRESS\_ISOLATION\_UNAVAILABLE}~\cite{adr0011}. This was exercised on one development host (live lane 3/3); it is containment-component evidence, not provider conformance.
\item \textbf{Policy refusal.} Under the committed generation-1 \texttt{v1alpha1} snapshot~\cite{bulletPolicy}, a fully valid grant with both blockers cleared still ends in \texttt{POLICY\_LIVE\_ADMISSION\_DISABLED} without spending its nonce~\cite{adr0011,adr0012}.
\item \textbf{Runner self-fence.} The runner keeps a local monotonic self-kill deadline at $\mathrm{now} + \tfrac{4}{5}\,\mathrm{TTL}$, strictly shorter than the server expiry, and freezes on the deadline or on a zero-row renewal; this is the STONITH direction of the fence-mediated liveness exit~\cite{kernelArch,adr0006}.
\item \textbf{BulletGit authority gateway.} In a fresh daemon, \texttt{clone} reaches the unavailable final-authority checker and returns \texttt{AUTHORITY\_CONTRACT\_UNAVAILABLE} before any repository I/O; later verbs fail \texttt{NOT\_CLONED}; the unsigned legacy token grants nothing~\cite{gitArch}. Hostile-git refusal, dissociate cloning under a mirror lock, scope and collision refusal, and preservation-before-cleanup are components (Section~\ref{sec:bulletgit}).
\item \textbf{Portal honesty.} One-time bootstrap to an HttpOnly/SameSite cookie plus CSRF value; exact HTTP~202 with a validated \texttt{PENDING} subject; every status read repeats the admitted command id; timeout and sequence gaps render \texttt{UNKNOWN} or \texttt{STALE}; eleven of fifteen catalog surfaces render explicit \texttt{unknown} because no farmd projection exists~\cite{portalArch}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Boundaries That Are Not Implemented}

Each item below is \ecD\ or \ecB. Naming them is the point.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Signed internal lease transport.} The existing \texttt{HttpLeaseClient} is unauthenticated, self-asserts scheduling and runner identity, and its release/advance are not full-subject atomic operations; it must not be mounted. A signed contract binding runner, authority epoch, request digest, and the full lease subject is a predecessor of any transaction~\cite{bulletClosure,adr0011}.
\item \textbf{S1/S2 containment.} ADR~0007's rootless-OCI S1 (namespaces, cgroup~v2, seccomp, read-only root, deny-by-default network) and separate-kernel S2 backends are designed; Landlock, seccomp, and namespaces are the intended Linux primitives~\cite{adr0007,landlock,seccomp,namespaces7}. The egress component above covers the network dimension only.
\item \textbf{Online mutation permits and settlement.} One-second PASETO mutation permits, Kernel online reservation and final check, and typed settlement are specified~\cite{adr0005}; BulletGit records the shape locally and refuses to proceed without them.
\item \textbf{Hidden holdouts (E3) and oracle-modifying diff classes.} Required for R2+ by C2; no holdout store or custodian exists~\cite{adr0009,centerrailRedteam}.
\item \textbf{Attestor $\ne$ broker.} The check-publishing path that recomputes a verdict from a signed proof bundle with its own credential (C4) is designed; live forges are blocked~\cite{adr0008}.
\item \textbf{Distinct OS identities per plane.} Required by ADR~0003; processes share a UID today.
\item \textbf{Kernel \texttt{v1alpha2} loader.} The hub validator accepts a generation-$\ge 2$ live-admission policy with a registered \texttt{provider-runner} key; the Kernel loader still accepts \texttt{v1alpha1} only~\cite{adr0012}.
\item \textbf{Content-addressed artifact storage, restore admission, and audit anchoring}~\cite{adr0009,kernelArch}.
\item \textbf{Same-UID pathname swap} between tool verification and Cargo/Node/npm exec during source setup is an accepted residual until a signed prebuilt installer exists~\cite{bulletRelease}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Formal Models: What the Two TLC Models Encode}
\label{sec:formal}

Exactly two protocols are model-checked in TLA+~\cite{lamport2002tla,yu1999tlc} and pinned by a model lock~\cite{bulletFormal} \ecC. The pin fixes the TLC release (1.7.4, tool version 2.19) by SHA-256 and SHA-1, requires Java~21, refuses a third module, and fails on any drift of module bytes, configuration bytes, or single-worker state counts. This section states what the models check so that no reader infers more.

\textbf{\texttt{LeaseFence}} models two runners, one lease, a monotonically increasing fence, a database clock, scope revisions with a mutation barrier, freeze, restore epochs, and apply attempts. Constants: \texttt{Runners = \{r1, r2\}}, \texttt{MaxFence = 3}, \texttt{MaxTime = 4}, \texttt{MaxScope = 3}, \texttt{MaxOps = 2}, \texttt{MaxEpoch = 2}, \texttt{MaxFreeze = 3}. The checked invariants are exactly \texttt{TypeOK}, \texttt{BarrierHasNoInFlightApply} (a scope barrier never coexists with an in-flight apply), and \texttt{RestoredAuthorityNeedsFreshAcquire} (no in-flight apply while no lease is held). Stale-fence, stale-epoch, expired, frozen, barrier, and unacknowledged-scope applies are refused by the \texttt{Authorized} guard and counted, not asserted as a separate invariant. Fence monotonicity holds by construction (the fence changes only in \texttt{Acquire}, by $+1$); it is not a checked property. Pinned run: 529,851 states generated, 128,913 distinct, depth~24.

\textbf{\texttt{EffectCheck}} models one effect and one dependent check through the phases absent, intent, dispatching, unknown, verified, orphaned, against a remote that may be untouched, desired, or mutated by a third party. Constants: \texttt{MaxDispatches = 2}, \texttt{MaxEpoch = 2}. The checked invariants are exactly \texttt{TypeOK}, \texttt{AtMostOneLogicalEffect}, \texttt{VerifiedEffectWasReadBack}, \texttt{CheckRequiresDurableProof}, and \texttt{ThirdPartyStateIsNeverAdopted}. A lost response leaves the effect in \texttt{unknown}; read-back adopts it only when the remote holds the desired state, marks it \texttt{orphaned} when a third party wrote, and returns to \texttt{intent} when nothing was written. Pinned run: 1,585 states generated, 378 distinct, depth~18.

What the models do \emph{not} do, stated so the limitation is citable:

\begin{itemize}
\item Deadlock checking is disabled in both configurations (\texttt{CHECK\_DEADLOCK FALSE}); no liveness or fairness property is checked. The models are safety-only and bounded.
\item Wound-wait, multi-resource scope expansion, and two-track amendment (A1, C3) are not modelled. The historical adjudication's own criticism---that a lock model showing only the no-conflict path proves nothing about deadlock freedom---still applies to the family's models, which make no such claim~\cite{potentialDraft}.
\item \texttt{AtMostOneLogicalEffect} is enforced by assignment: the logical-effect counter is set to one rather than incremented on each remote mutation, so the invariant cannot fail by construction. The real protection against a second physical write is the guard that dispatches only while the remote is untouched. A stronger model would count physical writes.
\item \texttt{EffectCheck} defines \texttt{NoDispatchAfterStop} (no dispatch while policy is expired or the system is frozen), but the configuration does not list it, so it is not checked.
\item Neither model represents Git, a forge, a provider, two Attempts on one Variant, or any cross-plane composition. They prove two protocols under small bounds. They do not prove the product~\cite{centerrailRedteam}.
\end{itemize}

The three JSON traces under \texttt{formal/traces/} are executable conformance fixtures replayed by Rust tests (\texttt{formal\_traces}, 2/2); they are not model-check results~\cite{bulletFormal}.

\section{BulletGit: The Missing Substrate}
\label{sec:bulletgit}

Models and harnesses commoditize. An agent-first source-control substrate that understands authority, change identity, proof, lineage, and protected integration does not~\cite{gitRole}. BulletGit is that substrate. \texttt{bullet-gitd} is the sole writer of the private workspace~\cite{gitArch}. Pack protocol, refs, and protected updates remain ordinary Git at the forge. Unless tagged otherwise, the mechanisms in this section are \ecC: they exist in \texttt{bullet-git-workspace} and \texttt{bullet-gitd} with mapped tests, and none is reachable through a production \texttt{clone}.

\subsection{Private Clone, Not a Worktree}

Workspaces never clone the operator's source tree directly. A bare mirror is created or fetched under an exclusive lock. The private clone runs
\begin{center}
\texttt{git clone --reference-if-able <mirror> --dissociate}
\end{center}
while the lock is held~\cite{gitArch}. Objects may be borrowed during the copy; no \texttt{alternates} file survives. Mirror GC cannot corrupt a live workspace. Immediately after clone, \texttt{origin} is removed, \texttt{credential.helper} is emptied, \texttt{GIT\_ASKPASS} points at a deny script, \texttt{GIT\_TERMINAL\_PROMPT=0}, and \texttt{GIT\_SSH\_COMMAND=false}. A model-issued push has no destination and no authenticator.

A \texttt{.git} \emph{file}---the on-disk shape of a worktree---is \texttt{WORKTREE\_FORBIDDEN}. Sequencer files at checkpoint time are \texttt{SEQUENCER\_ACTIVE}. Checkpoints run \texttt{git write-tree} against a temporary \texttt{GIT\_INDEX\_FILE}, never the live index.

\subsection{Hostile-Git Refusal}

The child environment is cleared of inherited \texttt{GIT\_*} variables and rebuilt with per-workspace \texttt{HOME}/\texttt{XDG}, \texttt{GIT\_CONFIG\_NOSYSTEM=1}, and \texttt{GIT\_CONFIG\_GLOBAL=/dev/null}~\cite{gitArch}. Every invocation disables paging, hooks, credentials, filesystem monitors, external attribute files, and signing, and passes \texttt{protocol.file.allow=never} except for the one local clone that needs file transport. Before a repository-scoped command, BulletGit parses local config with includes disabled. Command-bearing or truth-redirecting filter, alias, include, pager, URL rewrite, remote helper, diff/textconv, merge driver, submodule, credential, signing, editor, sparse, SSH, hook, worktree, or external attribute settings fail closed as \texttt{HOSTILE\_GIT\_CONFIG}. A real-repository test plants a clean filter and a canary behind \texttt{.gitattributes} and proves refusal before execution.

\subsection{Scope, Identity, Preservation}

\texttt{apply\_change} validates every path against a \texttt{ScopeGrant} before writing: Unicode NFC, no \texttt{..}, no \texttt{.git} component, no backslashes or NUL, no alternate data streams, no trailing dot or space, no symlink traversal or symlink targets. Duplicate and case-folded collisions refuse the whole batch; batches above 1,024 operations or 1~MiB per body refuse. \texttt{CandidateId} is content-derived from change, tree, and head with length-framed hashing, so two trees under one Change cannot share an identifier; \texttt{patch\_hash} is BLAKE3 of the \texttt{git diff base..head} bytes~\cite{gitArch}. A provenance-complete Candidate identity (distinct \texttt{ContentId}, complete proof manifests) is still open \ecD~\cite{bulletClosure}. Local cleanup accepts only an opaque, daemon-sealed preservation receipt that re-verifies source, destination, subject, journal, bundle, and full artifact immediately before deleting exactly the sealed work directory; a synced tombstone survives; post-delete ambiguity is typed \texttt{UNKNOWN}; online settlement is not a live path~\cite{gitArch}.

\subsection{Honest Production Boundary}

The public daemon constructor installs an unavailable final-authority checker. In a fresh daemon, \texttt{clone} returns \texttt{AUTHORITY\_CONTRACT\_UNAVAILABLE} before repository I/O~\cite{gitArch}. Later verbs fail \texttt{NOT\_CLONED}. The unsigned legacy token grants nothing. Production enablement still requires a published immutable \texttt{bullet-wire} tag, local PASETO verification, Kernel online reservation, and one-second operation permits~\cite{adr0005}. That fail-closed posture is a feature of the current tree, not a footnote.

\section{Evolutionary Control as Design}
\label{sec:evo}

Everything in this section is \ecD. The evolutionary document is normative for what ``evolutionary'' may mean in V1 and states explicitly that it is not a claim that any runtime property is complete~\cite{bulletEvo}. The committed policy pins \texttt{evolutionary\_authority=false}~\cite{bulletPolicy}, the closure plan gates the program on a real single-lane transaction, and the product-gap register lists it as G11, explicitly post-V1~\cite{bulletGaps}.

\subsection{What Evolves and What Cannot}

The unit of evolution is an immutable, content-addressed \texttt{TeamRecipe}, not a live agent. A recipe may name typed roles and their input/output contracts, communication edges and context-capsule policy, certified provider/model/profile choices, bounded concurrency, token, cost, time, repair, and verifier budgets, and deterministic stopping, escalation, dissent, fusion, and fallback rules. It cannot contain or evolve credentials, authority claims, safety rules, risk classes, protected paths, evidence floors, hidden evaluators, integration policy, effect permission, or hard budget ceilings. A new recipe creates new Attempts and workspaces; it never inherits a live session, credential, workspace, proof, or privileged cache from a parent~\cite{bulletEvo}.

Hard constraints are filters, never terms that a high score may offset. Campaign admission first applies a deterministic feasibility shield---authority, containment, provider/profile certification, role conflicts, quota, budget, verifier capacity, task-policy compatibility---and infeasible recipes consume no provider or verifier budget. Every task class retains a strong single-agent recipe as the incumbent and fallback; a multi-agent recipe earns eligibility only when matched evaluation shows that its surviving verified outcomes justify coordination, provider, and verifier cost. Agent count, transcript volume, confidence, and activity are never fitness dimensions~\cite{bulletEvo}.

\subsection{Archive, Ladder, Rollback}

Eligible results enter a bounded multi-objective quality-diversity archive in the MAP-Elites family~\cite{mouret2015mapelites,pugh2016qd}. Human-versioned behavioral descriptors may distinguish niches such as task/risk class, team topology, provider diversity, latency band, cost band, and verifier demand; descriptors must be observable from receipts and must not encode hidden-answer proxies. Within each cell, hard constraints filter first; the frozen policy then retains a Pareto set over independently measured correctness, robustness, cost, latency, intervention, and observation survival, with a stable content-ID tie-break. No global scalar reward can trade a safety regression for throughput. Archive cells, descriptors, objectives, and bounds are versioned policy subjects; changing one starts a new campaign rather than rewriting history.

Recipes advance only through comparable completed rungs, in the successive-halving spirit of staged budgets~\cite{jamieson2016sh,li2018hyperband,li2020asha}: B0 deterministic simulator, property, contract, and fault checks; B1 a small visible matched task block against the incumbent; B2 a larger visible block under the same total compute; B3 an untouched sealed confirmation under independent oracle custody; B4 no-effect shadow routing on live task classifications; B5 a bounded low-risk canary with automatic rollback; B6 an eligible routing arm with an incumbent traffic reserve. Pruning happens only between completed, comparable rungs. Raw holdout failures never enter author context, and a recipe family that authored a Candidate cannot provide its independence-required review~\cite{bulletEvo}.

Conservative routing chooses only among already certified recipes, records the eligible set and abstentions, and preserves a fixed incumbent reserve. One champion may coexist with multiple niche challengers. A sparse island model~\cite{alba2002islands} may copy only immutable recipe identity between campaigns, and the destination must re-evaluate it independently. Post-promotion monitoring covers escaped defects, revert rate, duplicate or ambiguous effects, false completion, cost, latency, verifier backlog, human intervention, and provider or task-mixture drift; a bound breach removes the recipe from routing, preserves lineage and negative knowledge, and falls back to the certified incumbent. Contextual routing learners~\cite{li2010contextual} are post-V1 and require holdouts, causal or offline evaluation~\cite{li2011offline}, rollback, drift detection, and an explainable signed decision record before influencing production admission. V1 does not update weights, prompts, policies, or privileges online.

\subsection{What We Must Not Lose From Each System}

Gas Town's factory metaphor is the right product skin for a fleet: named work, visible roles, escalation, and a merge rail an operator can watch~\cite{gastownReadme,gastownAudit}. Bullet Farm should keep those surfaces as projections over the event log, not as authority-bearing model personas. Gas City's primitive test---keep judgment out of transport, prefer primitives that remain useful as models improve---is compatible~\cite{bulletSnapshot}. The added Bullet rule is that transport success, task closure, session state, and model judgment cannot confer mutation, verification, or integration authority.

DeepSeek's session-log invariant---model-visible means logged---is the correct epistemology for a \emph{cognitive} plane~\cite{dshArch}. Bullet Farm needs the same discipline for prompts, tool schemas, and injected context. It must not use that log as repository truth. A reconstructable transcript proves what the model saw. It does not prove what landed on \texttt{main}.

Omnigent's L7 credential proxy is the correct shape for forge and provider secrets: the sandbox never holds the real token; the proxy attaches it on approved egress~\cite{omnigentBlog,omnigentPolicies}. ADR~0007 and the provider-admission crate already strip host secrets and project minimum OAuth copies~\cite{adr0007,kernelArch}. The remaining work is audited provider-only egress on every path, not a decision to put \texttt{GH\_TOKEN} back in the child environment. Omnigent's multi-device session sharing is a collaboration product. Bullet Farm's Portal is the opposite product on purpose: a sequence-bound projection with no skip-checks affordance~\cite{portalArch}.

Typical single-agent harnesses remain the right \emph{proposal producers}. V1 GA requires conformant Claude, Codex, Cursor, and Antigravity adapters under one proposal/evidence contract~\cite{adr0001,bulletClosure}. Wrapping them (Omnigent) or replacing their loop (DeepSeek) is a different layer. Bullet Farm's layer is the transaction around them.

\subsection{Interoperability Without Authority Leakage}

ACP and MCP are emerging interoperability layers~\cite{acpSpec,mcpSpec}. They should be consumed as capability observations and tool seams, never as mutation authority. A Cursor ACP session that streams a patch is a provider transcript. An MCP server that can run \texttt{git} is a hostile tool until it is reduced to a typed proposal or an admitted gate. The same rule applies to vendor ``plan'' modes: they are useful read-only observations. They do not admit a write.

\section{Identity, Quota, and the Human Seat}
\label{sec:identity}

This section is \ecD. Gas Town's scheduler is a rate governor (\texttt{scheduler.max\_polecats}) aimed at 429s~\cite{gastownReadme}. That is necessary and not sufficient. The Bullet Farm identity design names profiles with owner, authorization class, custody mode, credential generation, and health~\cite{potentialDraft,bulletEvo}. Personal CLI subscriptions stay in local custody; API and enterprise credentials use envelope encryption. Login success comes from an adapter \texttt{auth\_status} probe, never from a UI button. Quota observations are typed and ranked: official API, structured event, headers, typed limit, estimator, then \texttt{unknown}. \texttt{unknown} is never scheduled as headroom; the committed policy already fixes \texttt{unknown\_quota\_is\_headroom=false}~\cite{bulletPolicy}. A bounded probe reservation may convert unknown to known without scheduling bulk work on a guess.

Named human subscriptions are rate-governed to one seat-equivalent per human regardless of how many consumer logins an operator can collect; throughput must come from \texttt{api\_credit}, \texttt{organization\_seat}, or \texttt{service\_identity}. That rule is a compliance-as-code stance, not legal advice, and it is not enforced in the current kernel. It is recorded because it is the subsystem orchestrators most often omit.

\section{Evaluation and Falsification}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{What a Fair Benchmark Would Be}

A fair comparison uses one repository/task corpus, one tool budget, one wall-clock limit, the same provider profiles, the same retry cap, and the same integration policy~\cite{bulletSnapshot}. Report distributions, not a single success rate. Minimum metrics: in-window Candidate survival and protected-integration rate; escaped-defect and revert rate; false-completion and zero-test rate; duplicate or ambiguous effect rate; crash-recovery time and lost-work bytes; spend, elapsed time, and human interventions; verifier backlog p50/p95.

No benchmark begins while synthetic proof substitutes for the five-plane transaction. No public comparison is published without raw task subjects, configuration, receipts, exclusions, and the exact upstream commits in Section~\ref{sec:compare}. The first legal corpus is therefore: one Mission that materializes exactly once under kill-9 at every plane; one Variant that takes a never-reused fence; one private clone with no surviving \texttt{alternates}; one independent E2 reconstruction the author cannot weaken; one brokered effect whose timeout stays \texttt{UNKNOWN} until identity-exact read-back; and one portal snapshot that refuses to paint that \texttt{UNKNOWN} green~\cite{bulletClosure,bulletGaps}. Until that saga has a signed receipt, Tables~\ref{tab:authority} and~\ref{tab:git} are architectural comparisons, not performance results.

\subsection{What Would Reject the Design}

The design is rejected---not ``improved in a later version''---if any of the following is observed on an admitted build:

\begin{itemize}
\item a stale fence is accepted for a mutation, effect, or settlement;
\item one command materializes two live graphs or two writers in one checkout;
\item writer-produced proof satisfies an independent Evidence tier;
\item \texttt{UNKNOWN}, timeout, zero tests, or a skipped gate is stored or rendered as \texttt{VERIFIED};
\item a worktree, shared cwd, or surviving \texttt{alternates} file is admitted as a writer workspace;
\item the portal paints an unwatermarked or contradictory snapshot as a healthy empty list.
\end{itemize}

Those conditions are executable. They are the opposite of a scoreboard.

\subsection{What Would Falsify This Paper}

The design conditions above need an admitted build. The following conditions need only the commits in Appendix~\ref{app:pins} and the commands in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}, and each falsifies a specific sentence of this paper rather than the design:

\item Any \ecC\ row of Table~\ref{tab:inventory} whose mapped test command fails, or is skipped, at the named commit falsifies that row.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Transactions and effects.} Transaction processing is the ancestor~\cite{gray1981tp,gray1993tp}: identity, isolation, and recovery under crash. Sagas name why a long-running remote effect cannot be a local commit~\cite{garcia1987sagas}. Fencing tokens---a monotonically increasing number that the storage service itself must check---are Kleppmann's answer to a lock holder that pauses and resumes after expiry~\cite{kleppmann2016locking,kleppmann2017ddia}; Bullet Farm's never-reused fence, checked inside the ledger transaction and modelled in \texttt{LeaseFence}, is that idea applied to repository writes and remote effects. Idempotency keys make a retried request return the original result instead of a second effect~\cite{idempotencyKey,helland2012idempotence}; the \#5511 class is exactly a missing idempotency key on graph materialization. Wound-wait is the classic deadlock-avoidance rule the historical adjudication adopted for multi-resource scope expansion and the models do not yet encode~\cite{rosenkrantz1978}. Confused-deputy and capability discipline are why a portal, MCP server, or vendor sandbox must not inherit forge credentials~\cite{hardy1988deputy}.

\textbf{Tokens and canonical form.} PASETO v4.public is the signed-token format the wire uses, chosen over JOSE for its versioned, algorithm-fixed design~\cite{paseto}; RFC~8785 gives the canonical JSON that makes signed payloads byte-stable across Rust and TypeScript consumers~\cite{rfc8785}.

\textbf{Sandboxing.} Landlock restricts ambient filesystem and network rights for unprivileged processes~\cite{landlock}; seccomp filters system calls~\cite{seccomp}; user, mount, PID, and network namespaces isolate process views~\cite{namespaces7}; bubblewrap composes those into an unprivileged sandbox and is what Omnigent's native Linux wrappers use~\cite{bubblewrap,omnigentReadme}. Bullet Farm's committed policy names \texttt{linux-landlock-seccomp-cgroup-v1} as its production containment reference~\cite{bulletPolicy}; only the network-namespace egress component exists today.

\textbf{Merge queues.} The ``not rocket science'' rule---test the exact merge result before it becomes the branch---is the origin of Bors and its descendants~\cite{hoare2014notrocket,borsng,githubMergeQueue}; Gas Town's Refinery is a bisecting queue of that lineage. Bullet Farm keeps the rule and moves the authority: the forge's required checks decide, an attestor distinct from the delivery broker publishes them, and Evidence is bound to the exact Candidate rather than to a branch. Jujutsu shows why a stable Change identity matters and why a Git trailer is not enough~\cite{jjvcs}. in-toto and SLSA are the supply-chain vocabulary for the release artifacts Gas City already ships and Bullet Farm does not~\cite{intoto,slsa}.

\textbf{Formal methods.} The two models are TLA+ specifications checked with TLC~\cite{lamport2002tla,yu1999tlc}; Section~\ref{sec:formal} states their bounds.

\textbf{Search and selection.} The evolutionary design draws on quality-diversity archives and MAP-Elites~\cite{mouret2015mapelites,pugh2016qd}, on successive halving, Hyperband, and ASHA for staged budgets and early pruning~\cite{jamieson2016sh,li2018hyperband,li2020asha}, on island models for sparse recipe migration~\cite{alba2002islands}, and on contextual bandits with unbiased offline evaluation for the post-V1 routing learner~\cite{li2010contextual,li2011offline}. The family's addition is not an algorithm; it is the rule that hard constraints filter before any objective and that nothing in the genome can touch authority.

\textbf{Coding agents.} SWE-bench is the dominant issue-resolution benchmark~\cite{jimenez2024swebench}; SWE-agent, Agentless, OpenHands, and AutoCodeRover optimize an agent--computer interface or a no-agent scaffold for it~\cite{yang2024sweagent,agentless,wang2024openhands,autocoderover}. Multi-agent role graphs (ChatDev, MetaGPT, AutoGen) assign personas and conversation patterns, not incarnation-fenced write authority~\cite{qian2024chatdev,hong2024metagpt,wu2023autogen}. Indirect prompt injection through retrieved or repository-resident instructions is a documented confused-deputy class~\cite{greshake2023injection}. ACP and MCP are transport and tool seams~\cite{acpSpec,mcpSpec}. Gas Town, DeepSeek Harness, and Omnigent are the nearest \emph{product} neighbors and are treated as such in Sections~\ref{sec:taxonomy} and~\ref{sec:compare}, not as straw men. None of those systems binds Evidence to an exact Candidate OID.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Bullet Farm is pre-release~\cite{bulletRelease,bulletGaps}. There is no connected \texttt{TRANSACTION\_PROOF} at HEAD, so every composition claim in this paper is design. Live provider, Jeryu, and GitHub gates are operator-blocked. Hub-only installation is refused because the checked-in lock is schema~2. Security quality is below the V1 bar (hub Jankurai 58/raw58 with 10 caps and 44 findings: 29 high/hard and 15 medium/soft, at the 2026-08-25 audit~\cite{bulletRelease}; the figure moves with every audit). Eleven of fifteen Portal surfaces stay explicit \texttt{UNKNOWN}. Evolutionary multi-agent search is a normative document, not a running optimizer~\cite{bulletEvo}. C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C6b, C9--C12 are designed or out of V1; only C7 is a complete formal-methods receipt, and Section~\ref{sec:formal} lists the weaknesses of that receipt. The kernel test suite on the working tree reports three skipped tests; the committed receipt counts exclude them, and the family's no-skip-green rule applies to product profiles, not to those unit lanes~\cite{bulletTesting}. PostgreSQL, remote runners, and cross-repository sagas are scaffolds or absences. This preprint's authors are placeholders. External pins were re-verified by one reviewer on one date; Gas City's v1.4.1 release-note artifact list could not be re-fetched and is taken from the dated snapshot. Comparison cells for DeepSeek and Omnigent Git internals that are not stated in their public architecture are marked unknown rather than inferred from plugin names. The inventory HEADs are a dated snapshot; the working tree that compiled this PDF may be newer.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

The coding-agent industry is composing loops. Bullet Farm is a transaction processor for the thing those loops actually mutate: repositories and forges, under crash, timeout, and adversarial concurrency. Gas Town showed that roles, convoys, and a merge queue are valuable and that tmux, worktrees, and inferred completion are a ceiling. DeepSeek Harness showed that a session log and a plugin kernel make a single agent inspectable and recomposable. Omnigent showed that OS isolation, credential proxying, and cross-harness policy can be layered on top of the agents people already run. Bullet Farm keeps those product lessons and changes the substrate: private clones, incarnation fences, exact Candidates, independent Evidence, brokered effects, and a portal that would rather show \texttt{UNKNOWN} than a false empty success.

The components that exist today already enforce pieces of that contract and refuse the rest. The connected product is not done until the receipt table says so.

\section{Red-Team Self-Review}
\label{app:redteam}

A hostile reviewer will run the following attacks. Each is answered or conceded; the residual is stated.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{``Your Gas Town autopsy cites issues that do not say what you say.''} Conceded for one citation in earlier drafts, which attributed a respawn storm and a ``687 respawns in 24~h'' figure to Gas Town issue~742; that issue is about a settings file written to the wrong directory, and the figure has no primary source. This revision removed both and replaced them with issues~3584, 4737, and 4091, whose titles, states, and dates were re-fetched (Appendix~\ref{app:pins}). Residual: issue trackers move; the class survives even if any single report is fixed.
\item \emph{``Your implemented row is empty, so the architecture is vapor.''} Conceded for the connected product: nothing is above \texttt{COMPONENT\_PROOF}. Rejected for named component receipts: Table~\ref{tab:inventory} and Appendix~\ref{app:repro} are the contract with the reader, replayable at named commits.
\item \emph{``Two TLA+ models prove the system.''} Conceded. They prove two protocols under small bounds, safety-only, with deadlock checking off, one weak invariant, one unchecked invariant, and no wound-wait (Section~\ref{sec:formal}). They do not prove the product.
\item \emph{``You had a live end-to-end run and deleted it; either it worked or it did not.''} Both. The archived receipt shows the seven-clause transaction running once on real processes; the admission mechanism it ran under was an environment token that the family later rejected as not being admission, so the surface was removed and its replacement (signed grants, egress receipt, ratified policy) is not yet complete. Residual: the paper reports the receipt as \ecA, outside the ladder.
\item \emph{``Providers propose is unenforceable: the provider's own sandbox can write files.''} Partly conceded. The provider workspace generation is private and its bytes are never applied; only a validated \texttt{PatchProposal} reaches \texttt{bullet-gitd}. But S1/S2 containment is designed, only the network dimension is a component, and no transaction has yet composed admission, proposal, and apply. Residual: stated in Section~\ref{sec:threat}.
\item \emph{``A single-user loopback threat model is trivial.''} Rejected as trivial, conceded as narrow. The adversaries are the model, a concurrent incarnation, a retrying runner, hostile repository content, and a lying projection---the ones the issue corpus shows cause loss. Multi-tenant and remote-runner threats are named out of scope, not implied.
\item \emph{``You ship a security paper with 44 findings: 29 high/hard and 15 medium/soft.''} Conceded. The figure is reported, dated, and blocks release by the family's own rule; the paper claims no security quality.
\item \emph{``Omnigent's sandbox plus credential proxy is stricter than your V1.''} Conceded for OS-isolation maturity. Rejected as write-authority: the wrapped harness still runs Git and a policy cannot invalidate stale evidence.
\item \emph{``The bindings are just fencing tokens, idempotency keys, and merge queues with new names.''} Largely conceded, and cited as such in Section~\ref{sec:related}. The claimed contribution is the composition over repository subjects---fence at the effects gateway, Candidate identity from base/head/tree/patch, Evidence invalidated by subject change, preservation before destruction---and the refusal to let any model output cross those lines. Whether that composition is worth its machinery is a benchmark question this paper does not answer.
\item \emph{``An internal score or a green crate test is being sold as a result.''} Rejected. No internal weighted score appears; the only scores quoted are the historical adjudication's own (96.4/100 for the paper architecture, about 0.5/10 for implementation), reported as provenance. Every test count carries its subject and class.
\end{enumerate}

Further residuals accepted from earlier reviews: the eleven \texttt{UNKNOWN} portal surfaces are the correct projection of missing farmd routes, not unfinished UI; \texttt{v1alpha2} on HEAD does not mean live is on, because the Kernel loader accepts \texttt{v1alpha1} only and the committed policy is generation~1; the inventory HEADs are yesterday's tips for any later commit; the same-UID swap after verification is an open install hole (G1); \#5511 may be fixed upstream while the class remains; closing this preprint closed nothing in G1--G15; and a worktree with a unique branch is not isolation because \texttt{.git}, hooks, and the index are shared.
